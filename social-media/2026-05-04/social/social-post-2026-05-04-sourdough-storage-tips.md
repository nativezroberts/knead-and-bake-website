## Instagram / Facebook Caption (Long Form)

Can we talk about the plastic bag? 🛍️

We see it all the time — someone picks up a gorgeous loaf on Saturday, goes home excited, and immediately seals it in a zip-lock. By Monday it's gummy. The crust has gone soft and a little sad. And somehow they think the bread was the problem.

It wasn't. It was the bag.

Real sourdough is alive with lactic and acetic acids from a long, slow fermentation. Those acids are what give it that signature tang — and they're also natural preservatives. Your sourdough loaf is *built* to last, but only if you let it breathe.

Here's the rule we follow at our house: once you slice it, place the loaf cut-side down on a wooden cutting board and leave it there. No bag. No wrap. The crust seals the crumb, the board protects the base, and your bread stays fresh longer than you'd believe. A loose cloth bread bag works great too.

Never the fridge. Cold air pulls moisture from the crumb and accelerates staling in ways that are genuinely hard to reverse.

And the freezer? The freezer is your best friend. Slice before freezing, and you've got on-demand toast that comes back to life in minutes. A frozen slice of our Jalapeño Cheddar, straight into the toaster — honestly? Still incredible.

We put a lot of love into every loaf. A long overnight ferment, careful shaping, a hot oven. We want you to taste all of that work on Thursday, not just Saturday morning.

This week's post on the website breaks it all down — storage by variety, how to revive a slightly stale loaf, and why sourdough outlasts almost everything else in your bread box. Link in bio 🔗
