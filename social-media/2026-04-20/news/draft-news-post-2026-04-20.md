Every spring, as the Texas Hill Country warms up and neighborhood porches start filling with people, we notice something at the Castell Street Market: the Jalapeño Cheddar loaves go first. Sometimes before 9:15 AM.

## What Makes It Different From Jalapeño Cheddar Bread You've Had Before

Most jalapeño cheddar breads — even good ones — are quick breads or yeasted loaves. They're baked fast, the flavors sit on top of each other, and the cheese tends to get oily and flat.

Ours ferments for 24 hours before it ever sees the oven. That long cold ferment does something important: it mellows the raw jalapeño edge while concentrating the flavor, and it gives the cheddar time to weave into the dough rather than just sitting in it. The result is a loaf where you get a little jalapeño warmth on the back end — not a sharp hit up front — and a cheddar presence that's savory and deep instead of greasy.

The crust comes out golden and blistered, the crumb is open and chewy, and the whole loaf smells like something worth being excited about.

## How to Use It for Cinco de Mayo

We're big believers in bread as the anchor of a good spread. Here's what we'd do with a Jalapeño Cheddar loaf for a Cinco de Mayo gathering:

**As a centerpiece for dipping.** Tear it into chunks and set it out with a bowl of queso, black bean dip, or a bright avocado crema. The sourdough tang cuts through rich dips beautifully.

**As a sandwich base.** Slice it thick and pile it with carnitas, pickled red onions, and a smear of chipotle mayo. The jalapeño in the bread amplifies everything on top.

**Toasted with butter the morning after.** Honestly, this might be the best version. Day-old sourdough toasted in a cast iron pan with salted butter. Simple doesn't need to be improved.
